Crowns and bridges

Two ways to restore strength and appearance: a crown protects a tooth you still have, while a bridge fills the gap where a tooth is missing.

{{book_buttons}}

Crown or bridge

Crown

A cap that covers the whole visible part of a tooth, reinforcing it.

Often considered for
A tooth that is cracked, badly decayed or has a large filling.
What it does
Holds the tooth together and protects it from further damage.
Your own tooth
Stays in place underneath the crown.

Bridge

An artificial tooth joined to the natural teeth on either side of a gap.

Often considered for
Replacing one or more missing teeth.
What it does
Closes the gap so you can chew and smile with confidence.
Neighbouring teeth
Support the bridge, so their health matters.
{{img:crowns|frame--corner|A crown covers and strengthens a damaged tooth.}}{{img:bridge|frame--corner-br|A bridge fills a gap, supported by the teeth on either side.}}

The right choice depends on your teeth, so it starts with an examination.

Not every tooth suits a crown, and a bridge isn’t always the best way to replace a missing tooth. Your dentist will look at the tooth, the teeth around it and your bite, then explain the options, including dental implants where they may be suitable.
