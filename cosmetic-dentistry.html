What would you like to change?

Chipped or worn edges, gaps, discolouration, crowding. There is usually more than one way to treat each of these, from a simple clean and whitening to veneers or Invisalign.

  • Discoloured or stained teethProfessional whitening, or bonding and veneers where whitening won’t reach the cause.
  • Chipped, worn or uneven front teethComposite bonding for small repairs, porcelain veneers or crowns for larger changes.
  • Gaps between teethBonding, veneers, or Invisalign to move the teeth together.
  • Crowded or crooked teethInvisalign clear aligners, or braces by referral.
  • Old fillings or crowns that showTooth-coloured replacements.

Compare your options

Your options

Each treatment suits different concerns. The right choice depends on your teeth, your oral health and what you want to change, and your dentist will explain which ones suit you.

  • Teeth whitening

    Supervised whitening after a check for decay, cracks and gum disease, with a shade record so you can see the change. Results vary with the cause of the discolouration. Yellow tones respond best, grey and brown tones less. Fillings and crowns don’t whiten.

  • Composite bonding

    Tooth-coloured resin shaped onto the tooth to repair a chip, close a small gap, lengthen a worn edge or cover a mark. Usually one visit, little or no drilling, and reversible. Bonding stains and chips more readily than porcelain and is typically replaced or repaired every five to seven years.

  • Porcelain veneers

    Thin porcelain shells bonded to the front of teeth to change colour, shape, length and minor alignment. Veneers resist staining and usually last longer than bonding. Preparing a tooth for a veneer usually removes a thin layer of enamel, which is not reversible, so this is a decision to make with full information.

  • Invisalign

    A series of clear, removable aligners that move teeth gradually. Suitability is assessed first with a scan. Treatment times vary with the case. Aligners need to be worn most of the day to work, and retainers are needed afterwards to hold the result.

  • Crowns

    For teeth that are heavily filled, cracked or badly worn, a crown covers the whole tooth and restores its shape and strength. More tooth preparation than a veneer.

How it works

South Cairns Dental starts with a consultation, shows you what we see, explains each option including what it involves and what it costs, and lets you decide.

  1. Consultation

    Tell your dentist what you don’t like. They examine your teeth and gums, take photos and a digital scan, and check that your mouth is healthy enough for cosmetic work. Decay and gum disease are treated first.

  2. Options and costs

    You go home with a written plan showing the options that suit you, what each involves, how long it lasts, what it costs and what to consider. There is no obligation to proceed.

  3. Treatment

    Simple options can be done in one or two visits. Veneers, crowns and Invisalign run across several appointments.

  4. Looking after the result

    Your dentist will explain what each treatment needs to stay looking good, from avoiding staining foods after whitening to wearing retainers after Invisalign.

Realistic expectations

Cosmetic treatment can change the colour, shape and position of teeth, within limits set by your own teeth, gums and bite.

Your dentist will tell you what is realistic for your case before you commit, and will say so if they think a treatment won’t give you what you’re after. Results differ between patients and no outcome can be guaranteed.

If you are considering veneers, your dentist first checks your teeth, gums and bite and talks through whether whitening, bonding or another option could meet your goals. They can show you the planned shape and length using a mock-up on your teeth or on the scan, so you see the direction before anything is prepared.

Things to consider

Your dentist will explain the benefits, risks and alternatives that apply to you.

  • Veneers and crownsThey involve removing tooth structure and are not reversible. Teeth may be sensitive afterwards, and veneers and crowns can chip or come away and need replacing over time.
  • WhiteningIt can cause temporary sensitivity and does not change the colour of fillings, crowns or veneers.
  • InvisalignIt requires daily commitment. Teeth can move back if retainers aren’t worn.
  • Healthy teeth and gumsAll cosmetic work needs healthy teeth and gums underneath it and the same regular check-ups as natural teeth.

Questions patients ask

Which option is right for me?

It depends on what you want to change and the condition of your teeth. Bonding and whitening suit small changes and keep your options open. Veneers suit bigger changes in colour and shape. Invisalign suits position. Your consultation sorts this out.

Can I have a trial before veneers?

Your dentist can show you the planned shape and length using a mock-up on your teeth or on the scan, so you see the direction before anything is prepared.

How long does whitening last?

Typically one to three years depending on diet, smoking and how you look after your teeth. Top-up treatment is available.

Will veneers look fake?

Veneers are made to a colour and shape agreed with you. Most patients ask for a result that looks natural, and the mock-up stage is where that gets decided.

Does Invisalign work for adults?

Yes, provided the teeth and gums are healthy and the case suits aligners. Some cases are better treated with braces, and your dentist will say so.

Is cosmetic dentistry covered by my health fund?

Usually not for purely cosmetic items. Some restorative items may be. Check with your fund using the item numbers in your plan.
