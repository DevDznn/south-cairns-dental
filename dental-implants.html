Replacing teeth

Dental implants

An implant replaces the root of a missing tooth and supports a new tooth on top. It’s fixed in place, so there’s nothing to take out at night.

How an implant replaces a tooth

A dental implant has three parts: a post placed in the jawbone that acts as the root, a connector called an abutment, and a crown that looks like a natural tooth. Over the healing period, the bone bonds to the implant, giving it a strong foundation.

Implants can replace a single tooth, several teeth, or support a bridge or denture.

People often ask about implants when

  • A tooth has been lost or needs to come out
  • A bridge would mean preparing healthy neighbouring teeth
  • Dentures feel loose and they want more security
  • They want a fixed replacement that doesn’t come out

Is an implant right for you?

Implants aren’t suitable for everyone. Your general health, the bone in your jaw and the condition of your other teeth all play a part. If an implant isn’t the best fit, your dentist will talk you through alternatives such as a bridge or dentures, including implant-retained dentures.

Three stages, planned around you

Timing differs between patients. Your dentist will give you a plan for your own treatment.

  1. Consultation and plan

    Your dentist checks whether implants suit you and talks you through a treatment plan and costs. A mould of your teeth and a special 3D X-ray are taken before the next stage.

  2. Placing the implant

    The implant is placed into the jawbone under local anaesthetic.

    Healing, often around 3 months

  3. Your permanent tooth

    Once the implant has healed into the bone, your new tooth is fitted on top.

Benefits and things to consider

Your dentist will explain the benefits, risks and alternatives that apply to you.

Possible benefits

  • Fixed in place, without relying on neighbouring teeth
  • Looks and functions much like a natural tooth
  • Can help support dentures

Things to consider

  • Involves minor surgery and a healing period of months
  • Enough healthy bone is needed, and some people need extra procedures
  • Smoking, gum disease and some health conditions can affect success
  • Implants need daily cleaning and regular reviews, just like teeth

Alternatives to ask about

  • A bridge
  • Partial or full dentures
  • Leaving the gap, after discussing the effect on your bite

Looking after your implant

Always follow the instructions your dentist gives you for your own treatment.

  • After placementFollow your dentist’s instructions on eating, cleaning and pain relief while the area heals.
  • Every dayBrush and clean around the implant as carefully as your natural teeth.
  • ReviewsRegular check-ups help keep the gum and bone around the implant healthy.

Questions about dental implants

Am I too old for implants?

Age alone isn’t usually the deciding factor. Your general health, bone and gums matter more. Your dentist will assess this at a consultation.

How long does treatment take?

It varies. After the implant is placed, healing often takes around three months before the permanent tooth is fitted, and some cases take longer.

Cost and payment

Implant costs vary with the number of implants, the type of crown and any extra procedures. Your dentist will give you a written plan with costs after your consultation. Fees are always explained before treatment begins.
