Replacing teeth

Dentures

Whether it’s your first set or your old ones have stopped fitting, we’ll talk through the tooth replacement options that suit you.

Types of dentures

Full dentures replace all the teeth in the upper or lower jaw. Partial dentures fill gaps where some natural teeth remain, often clipping onto them for support. Implant-retained dentures attach to dental implants for a more secure fit.

Getting dentures usually takes several appointments, so they can be measured, tried in and adjusted until they look and feel right.

Broken or lost your dentures?

Call us. We treat broken and lost dentures as an urgent problem. Please don’t try to glue them yourself, and bring all the pieces with you.

Where are you starting from?

  • Old or loose dentures

    Dentures that rub, rock or slip can make eating and talking a chore. Your mouth changes over time, so a set that once fitted well may not now. We’ll check them and discuss your options.

  • Dentures for the first time

    If you’re missing several teeth, or teeth may need to be removed, we’ll explain what dentures involve and how they compare with other choices.

  • Implant-retained dentures

    Dentures can clip onto dental implants for a more secure fit. Ask whether this could suit you.

Benefits and things to consider

Your dentist will explain the benefits, risks and alternatives that apply to you.

Possible benefits

  • Replace several or all missing teeth
  • Help with chewing, speech and appearance
  • Can usually be adjusted or relined as your mouth changes

Things to consider

  • It takes time to get used to new dentures
  • The gums and jaw change shape over time, so the fit changes too
  • Loose dentures can cause sore spots

Alternatives to ask about

  • Dental implants
  • A bridge for one or two missing teeth

Looking after your dentures

Always follow the instructions your dentist gives you for your own treatment.

  • Every dayClean your dentures with a soft brush and a cleaner recommended by your dentist. Toothpaste can be too abrasive.
  • OvernightYour dentist may recommend taking dentures out at night to rest your gums. Keep them in water so they don’t dry out.
  • Your mouthBrush your gums, tongue and any natural teeth too.
  • Check-upsKeep having regular check-ups, even with a full denture, so your dentist can check the fit and the health of your mouth.

Questions about dentures

My dentures feel loose. What can I do?

Book a check. Your dentures may need adjusting, relining or replacing, or implant-retained dentures may be worth discussing.

How long until new dentures feel normal?

Everyone is different. It can take a few weeks to get used to eating and speaking. Come back if sore spots develop, as adjustments can help.
