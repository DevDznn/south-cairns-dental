Emergency dental care

We reserve appointments every day for dental emergencies. Call as early in the day as you can, tell us what’s happened, and we’ll do our best to fit you in. Please call rather than walking in.

Don’t wait with a dental emergency

Dental emergencies can be painful and distressing, and if left untreated they can get worse quickly. That’s why we reserve specific appointments every day for them. If you have a dental emergency, call us straight away. We’ll always try to see you as soon as possible.

You don’t need to be an existing patient, and we see children and adults.

A dental emergency may include

  • A painful tooth or gums
  • Broken teeth or fillings
  • Facial swelling
  • Trauma to your face, teeth or gums
  • A broken or lost denture

000

Call 000 or go to your nearest hospital emergency department

If there is difficulty breathing or swallowing, swelling spreading to the eye or neck, heavy bleeding that won’t stop, or a head injury such as loss of consciousness. Don’t wait for a dental appointment.

First aid while you wait

Simple steps that may help until you can see us. Your dentist’s advice always comes first.

  • Painful teeth or gums

    Toothache, throbbing, or pain when biting or with hot and cold.

    • Rinse gently with warm water.
    • Floss carefully in case food is trapped.
    • Pain relief from your pharmacy may help. Follow the label and check with your pharmacist that it suits you.
    • Don’t put tablets directly on your gum.

  • Broken teeth or fillings

    A chipped, cracked or broken tooth, or a filling that has fallen out.

    • Keep any broken pieces and bring them with you.
    • Rinse gently with warm water.
    • Chew on the other side and avoid very hot, cold or hard food.

  • Facial swelling

    Swelling of the gum, cheek or jaw, which can be a sign of infection.

    • Call us promptly. Swelling shouldn’t be left to see if it settles.
    • A cold compress on the outside of the cheek may ease discomfort.

    If swelling affects your breathing, swallowing or eye, or you feel very unwell with a fever, call 000 or go to hospital.

  • A knocked-out adult tooth

    A knock from sport, a fall or an accident.

    • Act quickly. Pick it up by the crown (the white part), not the root.
    • If it’s dirty, rinse it briefly in milk or water. Don’t scrub it.
    • If you can, gently put it back in its socket and bite on a clean cloth. Otherwise keep it in milk.
    • Call us straight away.

    Don’t try to put a baby tooth back in. Call us for advice. Any head injury needs medical assessment first.

  • Broken or lost dentures

    Dentures that have cracked, snapped or been lost.

    • Don’t glue or repair them yourself.
    • Keep all the pieces and bring them with you.
    • If a sharp edge is rubbing, leave the denture out.

  • Not sure if it’s urgent?

    If something doesn’t feel right, call and describe it. Reception can help you work out how soon you need to be seen.

What happens when you call

Have a pen handy. Reception may give you a time and some instructions.

  1. Tell us what’s happened

    When it started, where it hurts, and whether there’s swelling, bleeding or a broken tooth.

  2. We look for the soonest time

    We reserve emergency appointments each day and will try to see you as soon as we can.

  3. Your dentist relieves the problem

    The first goal is to treat pain or infection. If more treatment is needed, your dentist will explain the options for a later visit.

If we’re closed

  • Health advice any timeQueensland’s 13 HEALTH line is on 13 43 25 84, 24 hours a day.

Questions about an emergency appointment

Can you guarantee to see me today?

We can’t promise a same-day appointment, but we keep time free every day for emergencies and will do everything we can. Calling early in the day gives you the best chance.

Do you see children for emergencies?

Yes. We treat children and adults. Call us and tell reception your child’s age and what has happened.

I’m not a patient yet. Can I still call?

Yes. You don’t need to be an existing patient to call us about a dental emergency.
