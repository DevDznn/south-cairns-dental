Root canal therapy

When the nerve inside a tooth is badly inflamed or has died, root canal treatment can often save the tooth rather than removing it.

Call (07) 4055 4214In pain right now?

Why a tooth might need it

Inside every tooth is soft tissue containing the nerve. Two common causes of damage:

Deep decay

Decay that reaches the centre of the tooth can inflame or infect the nerve.

Injury

A knock or crack can damage the nerve, sometimes long before you notice symptoms.

What treatment involves

The number of appointments varies from tooth to tooth. Your dentist will explain the plan for yours.

  1. Assessment

    Your dentist examines the tooth and takes X-rays to see the roots.

  2. Cleaning the canals

    The damaged nerve tissue is removed and the canals inside the root are cleaned.

  3. Sealing the tooth

    The space is filled and sealed to help keep bacteria out.

  4. Restoring the tooth

    Your dentist will advise whether the tooth needs a filling or a crown to protect it.

Comfort

Worried about discomfort? Treatment is carried out under local anaesthetic. Tell your dentist how you’re feeling at any point; you can ask to stop for a break.

Saving your natural tooth

Root canal treatment removes the inflamed or infected tissue from inside the tooth, cleans the canals and seals them. The aim is to relieve pain and infection and keep your own tooth in place.

Not every tooth can be saved, and your dentist will explain the likely outcome for yours, along with the alternatives.

Signs a tooth may need assessment

  • Severe or lingering toothache
  • Pain that lingers after hot or cold food
  • Pain when biting or touching the tooth
  • Swelling or a pimple on the gum near a tooth
  • A tooth that has darkened after an injury

Whether it suits you depends on an examination by your dentist.

Weighing up your options

General information only. Your dentist will explain the benefits, risks and alternatives that apply to you.

Possible benefits

  • Can relieve pain caused by an inflamed or infected nerve
  • Keeps your natural tooth, which helps your bite
  • Avoids the gap left by an extraction

Things to consider

  • Treatment may need more than one appointment
  • The tooth may need a crown afterwards to protect it
  • Some teeth don’t respond and may need further treatment or removal

Alternatives to ask about

  • Extraction, then replacing the tooth with an implant, bridge or denture
  • Referral to a specialist endodontist for complex cases

Before and after your appointment

Follow the specific instructions your dentist gives you. They take priority over this general guidance.

  • Between appointmentsAvoid chewing hard food on the tooth until it’s fully restored.
  • AfterwardsTenderness for a few days is common. Call us if you have swelling or pain that gets worse.
  • Protect the toothHave the final filling or crown placed as recommended, so the tooth doesn’t crack.

Cost and payment

The cost depends on what your mouth needs, so it can only be worked out after an examination. Your dentist will explain the fees before treatment begins, and your printed plan lists what is recommended.

If you have private health insurance with extras cover, ask your fund what it will contribute. Fees and payment

Questions about root canal therapy

Is root canal treatment painful?

It’s carried out under local anaesthetic, and most people feel pressure rather than pain. The treatment is often what relieves the toothache.

Will I need a crown?

Back teeth in particular are often crowned after root canal treatment to protect them. Your dentist will advise on your tooth.

Can I just take painkillers and wait?

Pain relief may ease symptoms but doesn’t treat the cause. An untreated infection can spread, so please book an assessment.
