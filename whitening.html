Changing your smile

Teeth whitening

Professional whitening is a safe and effective way to brighten your smile. A specially designed whitening gel lifts discolouration that has built up over time.

What whitening can and can’t do

Professional whitening uses a bleaching gel to lighten the natural colour of tooth enamel. Results vary from person to person, depending on the cause of discolouration and your natural tooth shade.

Whitening doesn’t change the colour of fillings, crowns or veneers, and some types of staining respond better than others. Your dentist will check your teeth first and give you realistic expectations.

Benefits and things to consider

Your dentist will explain the benefits, risks and alternatives that apply to you.

Possible benefits

  • Can noticeably brighten natural teeth
  • Supervised by your dentist, who checks your teeth and gums first

Things to consider

  • Temporary sensitivity is common
  • Results fade over time, especially with coffee, tea, red wine or smoking
  • Not suitable for everyone, for example with untreated decay or gum problems

Alternatives to ask about

  • A professional clean to remove surface staining
  • Veneers for teeth that don’t respond to whitening

Before and after your appointment

Always follow the instructions your dentist gives you for your own treatment.

  • BeforeYour dentist may recommend treating decay or gum problems and having a clean first.
  • AfterwardsYour dentist may suggest avoiding strongly coloured food and drinks for a short time.
  • SensitivityIf your teeth feel sensitive, tell your dentist. A toothpaste for sensitive teeth may help.

Questions about whitening

Does whitening damage your teeth?

When you use a whitening product recommended and supervised by your dental professional, whitening is considered safe and doesn’t cause permanent damage to your teeth. Some systems sold online contain very high concentrations of bleaching agent that can damage teeth, and your dentist won’t recommend those. Your dentist also checks your teeth and gums first, so problems like decay or gum disease are dealt with before you start.

Will my teeth be sensitive?

Some patients notice mild sensitivity during whitening. It is temporary and returns to your normal level once you stop. If whitening trays are overfilled, the gums can briefly feel a slight burning and look white; this also settles quickly once the gel is rinsed away. Tell your dentist if your teeth are already sensitive.

How often will I need to whiten?

It varies from person to person, and depends a lot on diet and lifestyle. If you drink a lot of tea, coffee or red wine, or smoke, you’ll generally need to whiten more often. On average, a touch-up may be needed about every 6 months. Your dentist can suggest a schedule for you.

I have fillings or crowns on my front teeth. Can I still whiten?

Whitening works on natural tooth enamel. It doesn’t change the colour of fillings, crowns or veneers. Your dentist will discuss how this affects your result, and whether those restorations may need updating afterwards to match. Sometimes veneers are a better option.

Which whitening system do you use?

We use the Philips Zoom whitening system, which we believe is among the best available. Ask your dentist which option suits you.
