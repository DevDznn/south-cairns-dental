Repairing teeth

Wisdom teeth

Wisdom teeth don’t always cause trouble, and they don’t always need to come out. A proper assessment tells you which situation you’re in.

Why wisdom teeth can cause trouble

Wisdom teeth are the last teeth to come through, usually in the late teens or twenties. If there isn’t enough room, they can stay partly or fully under the gum, or come through at an angle. This is often called an impacted wisdom tooth.

A partly erupted tooth can trap food and bacteria, leading to inflamed gums and infection. Many wisdom teeth cause no problems at all, which is why assessment comes before any decision.

Swelling that spreads?

Swelling that spreads to your eye or neck, or makes it hard to swallow or breathe, needs urgent medical attention. Call 000 or see our emergency page.

Signs a wisdom tooth may be causing problems

If you notice any of these, book an assessment.

  • Pain at the back of the mouth
  • Swelling of the gum or jaw
  • A bad taste or bad breath
  • Infection around the tooth
  • Crowding of nearby teeth
  • Gum or tooth problems behind the wisdom tooth

Assessment comes first

  1. Examination and X-ray

    Your dentist examines your mouth and takes an X-ray to see how the wisdom teeth are sitting, including any that haven’t come through.

  2. A clear recommendation

    You’ll be told whether the teeth are likely to cause problems, and whether removal is advised.

  3. If removal is advised

    Your dentist explains the procedure, the costs and what recovery usually involves before you decide.

Benefits and things to consider

Your dentist will explain the benefits, risks and alternatives that apply to you.

Possible benefits

  • Removal can stop repeated pain and infection
  • Can protect the tooth in front from decay or damage

Things to consider

  • Recovery can involve swelling and discomfort for several days
  • Some cases are complex and may be referred to a specialist
  • As with any surgery, there are risks your dentist will explain

Alternatives to ask about

  • Monitoring with regular check-ups and X-rays
  • Treating a flare-up of gum inflammation and reviewing

If you have a wisdom tooth removed

Always follow the instructions your dentist gives you for your own treatment.

  • SwellingSome swelling is normal and often peaks after a couple of days. A cold pack on the outside of the cheek may help.
  • FoodStick to soft foods and avoid the area when chewing.
  • What to avoidSmoking, straws and vigorous rinsing in the first day or so.
  • When to callIf pain gets worse after a few days, you have a fever, or you notice a bad taste or swelling that is increasing.

Questions about wisdom teeth

Do all wisdom teeth need to come out?

No. If they’re healthy, in a good position and easy to clean, they can often stay. Your dentist will advise based on an examination and X-ray.

Can you remove them here?

Our dentists Alex and Louie have a particular interest in wisdom teeth surgery. Some complex cases may be referred to a specialist, and your dentist will discuss this with you.
